Learning: Tries

What is a Trie?

A Tree based data structure that is useful for storing strings in order, supporting fast pattern matching. Standard Tries are ordered such that the children of some Node are alphabetically ordered (so A before E and O before T, etc...). Additionally, the paths from the root to the leaf Nodes create a string.

How to Deal with Prefixes in Standard Tries?

When some words are prefixes of other words, without some way to deal with these shorter words, they get lost in Tries. Examples of prefix words: some and someone, won and wonderful, and bot and bottom. To deal with this, $ is a specical terminal symbol which makes sure that prefixes are clearly deliniated within a Trie. As such some$ and someone$ are both stored in a Trie.

Types of Tries

  • Prefix/Standard Tries: the discribed above Trie that is often used for autocomplete and wild card searching.
  • Compressed (PATRICIA) Tries: a compressed standard Trie. Instead of every letter being it's own node, internal nodes are compressed together, saving space. These are often done as such: ([word index], [start character], [end character])
  • Suffix Tries: store the suffixes of some word/words. As such, this works backwards from standard tries where letters are added from the back of the word to the front of the word. This allows for things like: substring search, finding longest common substring, and finding longest repeated substring.
